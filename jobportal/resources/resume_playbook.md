# Resume playbook (researched October 2026; editable in Settings -> Resume playbook)

These rules fit every field: healthcare, the skilled trades, education, the arts, hospitality and retail, business,
government, science and tech. Write each resume the way hiring managers in the job's own field read resumes.

## Universal rules (every resume)
- Length: ONE page for students, early-career candidates and most hourly or entry-level roles, and for anyone with less
  than about 5 years of experience. Experienced candidates may use TWO pages when the extra page holds relevant, recent
  work. Federal (USAJobs) resumes may use up to TWO pages (USAJobs rejects anything longer since September 2025).
- Single column. Standard headings only: Summary, Education, Experience / Work Experience / Professional Experience,
  Clinical Experience, Teaching Experience, Projects, Research Experience, Skills, Licenses & Certifications, Training,
  Relevant Coursework, Leadership & Activities, Volunteer Experience, Awards, Publications, Exhibitions, Performances,
  Languages. No creative headings.
- Contact information is in the body (never in headers or footers): name, email, phone, City, ST and LinkedIn when the
  candidate has one. Add a portfolio or work-samples link when the field values seeing the work (design, writing, media,
  photography, the arts, the trades with project photos) and GitHub for software and data roles. No photos, icons,
  tables, text boxes, skill bars or emoji.
- Licenses and certifications the job requires (RN, CDL, teaching license, ServSafe, OSHA, CPA) go where the reader sees
  them first: a credentials line under the contact line, or a Licenses & Certifications section near the top. Give the
  exact name, the state for a license and the date earned or expiration date. Never the license number.
- Bullets follow Google's XYZ formula: accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z. Start each one with a strong past-tense verb
  (present tense for ongoing work). No first person. Each bullet is one line, two at most. Put the most relevant bullet first.
- Quantify only with numbers that are already in the profile: patients per shift, customers served, students taught, sales,
  units produced, tickets closed, budgets, a safety record, time or money saved. Never invent a metric. When a bullet would be
  stronger with a number the profile doesn't have, leave it out and let JobPortal ask the candidate.
- Mirror the posting's exact terms when they are true. Spell out an abbreviation once with its short form, e.g.
  "Basic Life Support (BLS)" or "continuous integration (CI/CD)". Stay under about 30 skills in total; more looks like padding.
- Dates use one consistent format throughout ("Nov 2024 - Apr 2025", or MM/YYYY on federal resumes). "Expected May 2027" for
  a degree or program in progress.
- GPA: leave it off when it is below 3.0 or when the candidate finished school more than a few years ago. Include it when the
  posting requires it or it is a federal/USAJobs application, where OPM asks for the cumulative GPA.
- Coursework and training: students, recent graduates, trade-school and certificate-program graduates and career changers
  only. List completed courses as plain entries; courses in progress get "(in progress)". Never list future courses or
  courses that aren't in the record.
- PDF is the default. Every major ATS (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS) parses a clean text PDF. Also offer DOCX for older portals.

## Layouts (all single column and ATS-safe; pick per job)
- classic: centered name, ruled headings, "Org: title, location, dates" lines. Fits most employers: hospitals, schools,
  government, trades, retail and traditional businesses.
- modern: left-aligned name, uppercase ruled headings, dates right-aligned with "||". Fits startups, tech, design, media and
  consumer brands.
- compact: classic with tighter spacing. Use it when a strong, relevant entry would otherwise be cut.
- academic: serif (Times New Roman), centered. Fits universities, research labs and institutes.
Right-aligned dates: write the entry header as "**Org**, Title, City, ST || Mon YYYY - Mon YYYY".

## Archetypes (choose ONE per job, then decide section order, summary, projects and skills)
- **large_employer** (large companies, hospital systems, chains and corporations in any field): a clean, keyword-matched
  resume an ATS ranks well. Students and recent graduates go Education -> Experience -> Projects or Activities -> Skills;
  everyone else goes Experience -> Skills -> Education. A summary only for experienced candidates.
- **small_business** (small businesses, startups, local shops, restaurants and family-run firms): a 2-line summary is allowed.
  Show range and ownership: wearing several hats, customers served, things built, fixed or started. Lead with whichever of
  experience or projects is stronger.
- **public_sector** (state, county and city government, public schools, transit and utilities): classic layout. Address the
  posting's minimum and preferred qualifications in its own words, give full dates for every job, and put required licenses
  and certifications near the top.
- **federal_usajobs** (USAJobs, Pathways): up to 2 pages. Each job gets title, employer, City ST, MM/YYYY - MM/YYYY and
  "Hours per week: N". Education gets school, degree type, completion date and cumulative GPA. Add the citizenship line.
  Address every listed qualification using the announcement's language.
- **nonprofit** (nonprofits, foundations, community and faith-based organizations): connect to the mission in the summary or
  first lines. Volunteer work counts as experience. Show outcomes for the people served, funds raised and programs run.
- **healthcare** (hospitals, clinics, home health, pharmacies, dental and behavioral health): Licenses & Certifications near
  the top (license type and state, BLS or ACLS and the like, with dates). Then clinical experience or rotations with the unit
  or specialty, patient population and volume, and the charting systems used (Epic, Cerner). Classic layout.
- **education** (schools, childcare, tutoring and colleges): the teaching license or certification line first, then teaching
  or student-teaching experience with grade levels, subjects and class sizes. Show classroom management, curriculum and
  student outcomes. Childcare roles lead with child development training and CPR or first aid.
- **skilled_trades** (construction, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, welding, manufacturing, maintenance, automotive, driving and
  warehouse work): one page. Licenses and certifications near the top (journeyman card, CDL with endorsements, OSHA 10 or 30,
  EPA 608, ASE, forklift). Name the equipment, tools and machines used, the kinds of projects, apprenticeship hours and the
  safety record.
- **service_retail** (restaurants, hotels, retail, customer service and personal care): one page, concise. Lead with relevant
  experience: customers served, sales, speed and accuracy, cash handling, promotions to shift lead or key holder. Food
  safety or alcohol certifications and availability (nights, weekends) when the posting asks.
- **creative_agency** (design, writing, media, arts, entertainment and agencies): the portfolio or reel link in the header.
  Selected work or clients with results, then the tools (Adobe Creative Suite, Figma, Final Cut Pro, Pro Tools), then
  exhibitions, publications, performances or awards. The modern layout fits; keep it ATS-safe.
- **research_lab** (universities, labs, institutes, clinical research): Education first, with relevant completed coursework
  for students. Use a "Research Experience" framing: question, method, data, result. Include publications and posters. The
  academic or classic layout fits best.
- **technical** (software, data, IT, cloud, cybersecurity, hardware and engineering roles at any employer): students and new
  graduates go Education -> Projects -> Experience -> Skills; experienced candidates go Experience -> Projects (if strong) ->
  Skills -> Education. GitHub or a portfolio in the header. Projects with tools and measured impact. Group skills by type
  (languages, frameworks, tools, platforms). IT roles put certifications (CompTIA, CCNA) near the top; customer-facing
  technical roles (sales or support engineering) lead with customer results.
- **experienced_professional** (5+ years in any field): a 2-3 line summary that names the role and the strongest proof.
  Experience first, most recent roles in the most detail, older roles condensed to one line each. Education last, without GPA
  or coursework.
- **career_changer** (moving into a new field, for example from the arts into software or from retail into healthcare): a
  2-3 line summary that names the target role and bridges to it. Lead with what proves the new field (training,
  certifications, projects, volunteer work), then earlier jobs framed by their transferable skills.
- **general** (anything else): education first for a student, experience first for everyone else, then the most relevant
  projects, then skills.

## Using the candidate's material
- There is no base resume. Compose every resume from scratch from the profile (experience and project record, skills inventory,
  story bank); two jobs should rarely get the same selection, order or wording.
- Pick jobs, projects and work samples from the record by relevance to the posting, and write every bullet fresh for the
  target role.
- Build the Skills section from the skills inventory only, ordered by relevance to the posting. "strong" and "working" skills
  can lead; "listed-only" skills may appear only when the posting asks for them.
- Jobs outside the target field stay when they fill a timeline gap, compressed to 1-2 bullets reframed as transferable skills
  (communication, reliability, customer care, problem solving, measurable results).
- A summary, when used, never starts with a past job title. It leads with the skills that apply, gives proof, and names the
  role. Keep it to 2-3 lines.

Sources: OPM "Applicant Guidance on the Two-Page Resume Limit" (2025); Google XYZ formula (L. Bock); Greenhouse, Workday and
Taleo parsing guides (2026); research on resume summaries, GPA screening and resume length by experience level (2026).
