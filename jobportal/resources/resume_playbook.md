# Resume playbook (researched October 2026; editable in Settings -> Resume playbook)

## Universal rules (every resume)
- Length: ONE page for students and anyone with less than about 5 years of experience. Experienced candidates may use TWO pages
  when the extra page holds relevant, recent work. Federal (USAJobs) resumes may use up to TWO pages (USAJobs rejects anything
  longer since September 2025).
- Single column. Standard headings only: Summary, Education, Experience / Work Experience / Professional Experience,
  Technical Experience, Projects / Technical Projects, Research Experience, Skills / Technical Skills, Certifications,
  Relevant Coursework, Leadership & Activities, Volunteer Experience, Awards, Publications. No creative headings.
- Contact information is in the body (never in headers or footers): name, email, phone, City, ST, LinkedIn, plus GitHub
  and/or a portfolio when the role values work samples. No photos, icons, tables, text boxes, skill bars or emoji.
- Bullets follow Google's XYZ formula: accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z. Start each one with a strong past-tense verb
  (present tense for ongoing work). No first person. Each bullet is one line, two at most. Put the most relevant bullet first.
- Quantify only with numbers that are already in the profile (counts, percentages, time saved, users, revenue, scale).
  Never invent a metric. When a bullet would be stronger with a number the profile doesn't have, leave it out and let
  JobPortal ask the candidate.
- Mirror the posting's exact terms when they are true. Spell out an acronym once with its abbreviation, e.g.
  "continuous integration (CI/CD)". Stay under about 30 skills in total; more looks like padding.
- Dates use one consistent format throughout ("Nov 2024 - Apr 2025", or MM/YYYY on federal resumes). "Expected May 2027" for a
  degree in progress.
- GPA: leave it off when it is below 3.0 or when the candidate graduated more than a few years ago. Include it when the posting
  requires it or it is a federal/USAJobs application, where OPM asks for the cumulative GPA.
- Coursework: students and recent graduates only. List completed courses as plain entries; courses in progress get
  "(in progress)". Never list future courses or courses that aren't in the record.
- PDF is the default. Every major ATS (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS) parses a clean text PDF. Also offer DOCX for older portals.

## Layouts (all single column and ATS-safe; pick per job)
- classic: centered name, ruled headings, "Org: title, location, dates" lines. Fits general, government and traditional employers.
- modern: left-aligned name, uppercase ruled headings, dates right-aligned with "||". Fits tech, startups and consumer companies.
- compact: classic with tighter spacing. Use it when a strong, relevant entry would otherwise be cut.
- academic: serif (Times New Roman), centered. Fits research labs, institutes and universities.
Right-aligned dates: write the entry header as "**Org**, Title, City, ST || Mon YYYY - Mon YYYY".

## Archetypes (choose ONE per job, then decide section order, summary, projects and skills)
- **big_tech_swe** (large software companies): students and new grads go Education -> Projects -> Experience -> Skills;
  experienced engineers go Experience -> Projects (if strong) -> Skills -> Education. No summary for students. GitHub in the
  header. Deep projects with stack and impact. Group skills as Languages / Frameworks / Tools.
- **startup_product** (startups, consumer apps, mobile): a 2-line summary is allowed. Lead with what was shipped (projects or
  experience, whichever is stronger). GitHub, App Store and live links before LinkedIn. Use words like shipped, ownership,
  velocity and real users when true.
- **defense_contractor** (defense and government contractors): put "U.S. Citizen | Eligible for Security Clearance" (or the
  actual clearance) directly under the contact line when true. Use the exact requirement keywords and any standards or tools
  named in the posting. Classic layout. An optional 1-2 line summary that maps to the program.
- **aerospace_space** (aerospace, satellites, new space): add the citizenship line when the role is export-controlled (ITAR).
  Lead with hands-on hardware and software work written with engineering rigor: scope, method, measured outcome, constraints.
- **research_lab** (universities, national labs, institutes): Education first, with relevant completed coursework for students.
  Use a "Research & Technical Projects" framing: question, method, data, result. Include publications and posters. The academic
  or classic layout fits best.
- **federal_usajobs** (USAJobs, Pathways): up to 2 pages. Each job gets title, employer, City ST, MM/YYYY - MM/YYYY and
  "Hours per week: N". Education gets school, degree type, completion date and cumulative GPA. Add the citizenship line.
  Address every listed qualification using the announcement's language.
- **data_ml** (data science, analytics, ML and AI engineering): projects and experience with datasets, models, measurable results
  and tools. Skills split as Languages / ML & Data / Infrastructure. Statistics and data coursework for students.
- **it_support_infra** (IT, help desk, sysadmin, DevOps, cloud): hybrid format, with technical skills and certifications near
  the top. Lead with troubleshooting, systems, networking and tooling. Frame customer-facing jobs as technical support when true.
- **solutions_sales_eng** (solutions, sales, customer and field engineering, technical account management): a summary is
  allowed. Experience comes first when customer-facing results are the differentiator (deals, accounts, revenue, adoption).
  Follow with technical projects that prove depth.
- **experienced_professional** (5+ years in any field): a 2-3 line summary that names the role and the strongest proof.
  Experience first, most recent roles in the most detail, older roles condensed to one line each. Education last, without GPA
  or coursework.
- **general** (anything else): education first for a student, experience first for everyone else, then the most relevant
  projects, then skills.

## Using the candidate's material
- There is no base resume. Compose every resume from scratch from the profile (experience and project record, skills inventory,
  story bank); two jobs should rarely get the same selection, order or wording.
- Pick jobs and projects from the record by relevance to the posting, and write every bullet fresh for the target role.
- Build the Skills section from the skills inventory only, ordered by relevance to the posting. "strong" and "working" skills
  can lead; "listed-only" skills may appear only when the posting asks for them.
- Jobs outside the target field stay when they fill a timeline gap, compressed to 1-2 bullets reframed as transferable skills
  (communication, troubleshooting, ownership, measurable results).
- A summary, when used, never starts with a past job title. It leads with the skills that apply, gives proof, and names the
  role. Keep it to 2-3 lines.

Sources: OPM "Applicant Guidance on the Two-Page Resume Limit" (2025); Google XYZ formula (L. Bock); Greenhouse, Workday and
Taleo parsing guides (2026); research on resume summaries, GPA screening and resume length by experience level (2026).
